Emberheim

The years

The Empire of Emberheim rules from Ironspire. Its banner is a compass rose set before a roaring flame, with a fanciful E in the center. The empire's business is invention. Condensed mana, liquid mana, firearms, and clockwork sit beside churches, bounty boards, and desert towns that still run on horses and bad whiskey.

Liquid mana was discovered in 300. It sells at 50 gp an ounce. The capital runs on condensed mana. Dusthaven grew up around an oasis of the same stuff in the western desert. Aetheria, an older sun-worshipping civilization, rose in 303 and fell in 686.

Ironspire

Ironspire is the capital: constant invention, metal, and mana. The Empire protects its citizens here with breakthroughs in condensed mana. Magitech inventors run through a city that seems made of metal and steam. Emberheim exports creations and imports raw materials. It is a hungry town.

Most buildings are metallic spears shoved up through the steam that clings to the streets like fog. None of them may rise above the Imperial Palace. The Council meets there to pass laws, order the military, and approve new inventions.

The stolen schematic, the gigs, the sack of cold, the contest, and the audience are in Weird West.

Mundane

A smaller town on the central continent, north of Ironspire, in wooded hills that are just beginning to turn cold. A small sea lies between it and the Enigma. It is where people go to hone traditional magic, and the railway north ends beyond it at Glacier's Edge, about 125 miles, a day at 12 hours 30 minutes, 10 gp.

The College of Mundane

The school of the town. Anybody in the world may come looking for knowledge of magics. No masters, tuition, or library list are written.

Dusthaven

GM. Elowen Sunweaver founded Dusthaven in 906. She is an elven archmage of fire and sun, now a lich, living deep in the Enigma. The town does not know she founded it, does not know where she is, and does not know she is a lich. Those three facts have always been kept secret. Her tower is now the mayor's office, and her sealed journals are still in it. See People and Aetheria.

What Dusthaven tells is the town sheet's story: decades ago a human explorer found an oasis of condensed mana in the far west of Emberheim, in an unforgiving desert, and the town exploded out of it. The place is rough, full of beasts and bandits, and famous for bounty hunting. The Sheriff's Office posts outlaws, rogue magitech inventors, and the irregular creatures that threaten the town.

The Weird West company now knows more than the town does, and less than the truth. They know Elowen created the temple that housed the Sun Prism, and that she is a powerful wizard. They do not know she founded the town, where she is, or that she is a lich. The current mayor is Thorian Brightshield.

Named locations: Sheriff's Office, Gunther's Guns, Spare-a-drop Supplies, the Lounging Lizard Inn, the railway station, the stables, and the church.

The Marauder war, in order, is in Weird West. The cave they used while Zephyr and Sirocco led is in Miscellaneous locations.

Steamwood

Steamwood was built to house engineers and trains, then became a workers' town. Construction brought the other blue-collar trades with it. It is still the nation's largest exporter of food and raw resources: farming, mining, logging. It sits in unexplored forest. People walk into the woods after a valuable rumor and are not heard from again.

The approach is the train: the ground goes green, the trees close in, the brakes scream, and the town is rough buildings for use rather than beauty, strung along a creek and running out of sight into the woods.

Named locations: the Axeman's Alehouse, the Steamwood Central Market, a general store, the stables, the church, and the railway station. The general store sells the ordinary list in Mundane goods. The Shadow

All of this sheet is player-visible text. It is the revenge plot after Highrest.

The party returns to Highrest at night. Thick low fog, cold moonlight, quiet streets, few windows still lit. The unfinished fountain statue, their faces just beginning to be cut, is stained wet crimson. Corpses fill the red basin: two men dressed like Az and Khan, one woman dressed like Wendy, and the mangled corpse of a cat and a frog on top. A man in a dark, gold-trimmed cloak floats face down. The cloak is unmistakably Caelric's. Turned over, the face is not Caelric's. It is a random man. Carved into the open chest: SOON.

Up the muddy street, glass breaks and a woman screams. Shadows drag her into the road. She flails. The life drains. Her skin desiccates onto the bone and she stops. Shadows like snakes pour from the alleys, a horde, barely visible by moonlight.

Paul Marauder's grave has been desecrated. A large smiley face is drawn on it in blood. He died in Castle Darkstone and was buried here.

At the inn they were promised, Caelric is gone. His window is shattered. He fought. The room is a mess. Blood, both crimson and blackened. A couple of gold coins near the window. Waist-deep murky gray water. A few floating patches of purple fungus light the room. In the center, a statue of a robed humanoid with no face. The body looks composed of long strands gathered into limbs. It holds a stone dish with an extremely shallow layer of dirt. East, a large arch and closed stone doors. Something is written over the arch in a strange runic script.

In Undercommon, the outside reads as a warning to return the way you came. It also reads Altar of Rot.

Anything set in the dish rots fast, becomes dirt, and filters into the statue. Anything living that touches the plate takes 7 (2d6) necrotic damage. Enough dirt and the hands lower, a single Worm of Kyuss shoots out, and the doors open.

Scratched on the wall, almost looking like natural stone, in Thieves' Cant: "Starving, durt hurts, stupid worms." The spelling "durt" is the sheet's.

The Ninth Circle (Treachery)

GM, the cosmology. A giant frozen lake. An empty icy wasteland as far as anyone can see, and stinging snowy wind that cuts that distance down. Inside the ice are the souls of people who betrayed those closest to them, forever reaching for whatever they sinned to get: wealth, women, or power. Beasts and devils roam, and they betray each other for scraps. Deep under the ice is the center of Hell, and Satan. Velkhana reigns over the stretch of ice where Krampus's portal came in. The Sack of Cold job out of Ironspire ends with Velkhana dead and one of two Velkhana crystals in the party's hands.

Three encounters are written apart from the scenes: succubi drift on the Ethereal Plane and wait for lost souls to sleep, so they can charm them and betray them. An ice devil patrols with a chained yeti, looking for defiant souls and rowdy beasts to press into the ice. A pair of frost giants wander, hunting. Lost souls look like enough of a meal.

Arrival. The party crawls through a canvas sack. It tightens until it feels as if it is squeezing the breath out. The air gets colder. Each breath hangs as mist too long. Then the sack drops away.

A world of blinding white. A massive frozen lake under a pale light with no source. Just under the ice, bodies in grotesque contortions, hands out. Each one reaches for something just past the fingers: gold, the outline of a lover, an intangible crown. Mouths frozen mid-scream. A frigid wind, and the faint venomous whispers of the damned. Somewhere, chains drag. Now and then a wail that is colder than the frost.

On the horizon, a jagged mountainous spire of glacial ice. Looking at it puts a dark pressure on the heart. It pulls.

Behind the party, a small faintly glowing portal hangs just over the ice, edges like disturbed water. Through it, the dim glow of the church. The portal already looks impossibly small, as if the sack is trying to close it, and the glow fades by the second.

A shadow sweeps the lake. The light dims. The cold bites harder. The ground trembles. A massive winged creature of ice, wings blotting out what is above, snow falling off every beat. A low growl in the chest. Glowing soulless eyes sweep the ice, hunting. Then it is gone over the horizon. This is the sighting. The sheet does not name it in the scene. The GM note names Velkhana as the ruler of this stretch.

The giants. Two massive shapes. Frost-covered skin, snow-white beards, tattered cloaks of frozen hides, pale blue eyes. Between them, a makeshift lantern of rock and bone. Inside, a crystal gives a faint golden warmth. One speaks, mournful, like distant thunder: they bear warmth where none exists. The giants want a gift, a memory of warmth, love, or joy, to feed the light. Without it the cold claims the giants. The smaller one adds that if the party refuses, the giants must survive, and the party's warmth will do. The lantern flickers. Desperation and regret.

The same bargain is the mechanic of the Shard of Warmth in Az's gear: speak a warm memory into the crystal, lose that memory, charge the shard.

The traitor under the ice. The wind carries sorrowful whispers. Cracks draw the eye down. A body just under the surface, hand up. The eyes move. The mouth moves with no sound. Then, in the mind: walk free while he stays bound, and he can help. Free him and he gives wealth, power, knowledge. All he asks is mercy.

If freed, the body claws up, almost liquefying to squeeze through, breaks out, looks at the sky, breathes, then flies at the rescuer and jumps inside. A Wisdom save or 3d6 psychic. The mind goes numb. New memories: a knife in an unsuspecting back, poison in a celebratory drink, adultery behind a close friend. "Learn from my mistakes, know the signs. I thank you." The boon is advantage on Insight checks to detect deceit, and a grown want for wealth. The sheet says "you expect more, however that may be achieved." Remove Curse can take the boon off.

If denied, he curses the party to belong in the ice with the rest. More figures stir under the ice, clutching bloodied gold, stolen hearts, broken blades. Together they ask, inside the mind: "Who among you can be trusted?" The effect is constant suspicion of anyone close, an inability to trust, and disadvantage on Wisdom saves against being charmed, until Remove Curse.

The stair. At the base of the spire, a giant crack in the spire and the ice makes a wide rough staircase into the solid lake. Not a long trip down. The stairs end at a short cavern. Forward is a wall of fog and snow.

The beast. Stepping out of the cave, the fog is gone. The howling wind is gone. An eerie silence. The ice feels fragile. Cracks spider from where the party stands. For the first time the frozen hell is visible: mountains of jagged ice like shattered teeth, glaciers glinting. For Khan, the landscape is not the thing that stops the breath. Miles away, something that seemed to be a hill of frost rises. Its shape is barely beastlike, and it locks eyes. Daggers of ice strike the heart, the stomach drops, and it is time to leave.

Back to the portal. Farther from the spire, the ice weakens. Large cracks. Desiccated souls try to crawl through. Steps start to splash in shallow puddles. The entry portal is a small circle of warm light over a chunk of ice still standing in the shallows. Souls clutch from the water, pull themselves up, hold each other down to use the bodies as leverage. Some who got out grapple each other. Some float toward the pale light. A pair see a metal glint. Hollow sockets full of greed. They clamber over each other toward the party.

Sleep. A succubus. Wisdom save. Hard to sleep. Nightmares of greed, of friends cutting a throat while the dreamer lies there. Then awake, still in the icy cave, standing, weapons ready. An incomprehensible beast towers in front of the dreamer. The party is at their side, and Val. Val shouts to get ready, says she has their backs, as always, holds up her cross, and prays. Divine magic swirls, lifts her habit, and the dream lingers on her. Val is not Queen Beatrice, and she is not Valentina. Valentina is the illusion in Aric's bed. This Val wears a habit and a cross.

The Elemental Plane of Air

Carter Wint opened this road. In 973 he found a Node of Air in the Sea of Sand. In 974 he met the aarakocra, learned the name Yan-C-Bin, and started bringing Auran work home. In 975 Yan-C-Bin attacked Shakharra, killed many, and possessed him. Carter retreated to his castle, and from that year Yan-C-Bin has a physical body. The present company sees the cloud islands on summer 21–22, before they ever reach Evershroud.

Shakharra

The only remaining town of the aarakocra, and it stands in the Elemental Plane of Air. A fortress on a gargantuan floating island. Wooden docks ring a small harbor on the south peninsula. Floating wooden carts are parked along those docks. Lord Kaarak rules it. His name is said kay-rack. The Flying Boats company only glimpses the place during the summer of the cloud islands. In 975 Yan-C-Bin attacked it, killed many, and possessed Carter Wint there.

CW's Hidden Laboratory

Hidden deep underwater. Carter can reach it because he does not need to breathe. A huge stone door sits at the bottom of a deep lake, barely lit, the letters CW carved into algae-ridden stone. The last key was entrusted to Lord Kaarak.

The key touches the door and the stone becomes a light yellow force field. Through it, a large dry room. Glowing orbs line the walls, low and jarring, and the light leaks into the water. Walking through drops a person to the floor. Buoyancy is gone. Clothes and body are dry. The stone is roughly carved into a huge circle, with alcoves of tables, chairs, and shelves: metal, stone, crystal, and dozens of books.

First Node

Three islands, reached from a node.

Mined island. Small, mostly crag. Footing on the north end, then a tunnel to an open quarried middle. A flooded cavern goes deeper, propped with old water-damaged wood. Anything wild in there collapses it. A feral water elemental lives in the water. At the top of the caverns, a small wooden room. The furniture and papers are waterlogged and strewn. One note is hammered above the door. One chunk of Aureleonite is under the dirty water in that room. The sheet spells it Aurelionite. More could be mined deeper in, if the cavern holds.

Couatl island. A snaking island, lush on top, two small crags floating beside it. A river winds through the middle, drops down short cliffs, and falls off into mist. Couatls fly lazy between the trees and above them. Some rush in, curious, shapeshift to mimic the company for fun, and mutter Auran in their minds. Behind the tallest waterfall is the nest. Among the sleeping couatls, piles of sticks, grasses, and leaves hold silver, gold, and crystal. If Wint is mentioned, they fetch a stone orb with white glowing lines spiraling on it.

Node island. Large, sparse forest and tall grass, then a sheer cliff. Below the cliff the rock is worked stone, and a huge double door is set in the wall. A small rounded basin stands in front. Smooth twirling runes arch over the doors: "Unlock these doors only if you wish to guard this portal with your life."

The key goes in. White light climbs the walls and the seam. The doors swing forward on a gust of stale air. Inside, a circular room carved in the island, a soft white glow between the floor tiles. Skeletons slump along the walls in tattered rags, old spears in their hands. They were not human. Each skull has a large beak, and bony wings lie behind them. One wears armor and sits a stone throne across the room. The doors begin to shut.

The skeletons rise and do not advance. They ready their weapons. A moment of stillness, then they charge. When the last bones drop, white light surges through the floor. The thick stone column in the center hums, shoots a beam into the ceiling, and the beam hits a giant crystal, flies into the doors, and pushes them open. It dissipates into clouds a little off the island. The clouds congeal, become a crystalline mass, and shatter outward into a rapidly spinning circle. Through it: a rough sea, a low fog, and many small lights in the distance.

As the ship pushes for the portal, figures rush past from inside the island. Masses of cloud, faces darkened with anger, and in front a human in gleaming metal armor. Streaks of white light swirl on his flesh. He flies with nothing holding him up, fists forward, one hand on a longsword, and he looks back grinning. His eyes are glowing white. They go straight for the portal.

On the far side the cloudy creatures shrink. Some pop into several smaller ones. Only the largest two keep their shape. The humanoid slows, staggers in the air, and the light in him dulls. He keeps on, straight for the lights.

That crossing is the summer of 21–22. The ritual is beaten, a node home opens for a moment, and the Auran agent who comes through near Azure Lagoon is killed at once. He names Yan-C-Bin before he dies.
