#### Miscellaneous locations

Sites reached from Emberheim that are not a town and are not printed on the map.

##### The Orecrusher Vault

**GM.** Clive asks a fair share of the loot and, in return, leads the party and helps as he can. The undead who chased him are blamed, on his sheet, on local necromancers.

An easy-to-miss crack in a cliff opens onto a natural chamber. The far wall is built. A massive stone door, intricate dwarven runes, a hum of arcane energy. Beside the entrance, a small stone dragon with its mouth open. The riddle:

> I'm sought by many, prized by all, Those who have me, use me well. Forged in the earth, yet born of fire, I'm the key to what you desire.

Gold placed in the dragon's mouth starts the mechanisms. Stone slides aside. A carved path, unlit torches, stale air.

The first room is a floor of symbolic runes. A crumbling skeleton lies on one tile. The far arch is a verse in Dwarven:

> In the forge's fiery dance, a tale unfolds, Hammer's rhythm, a story of swords untold. Steel whispers secrets, the anvil sings, A warrior's blade, from the heart of the forge springs.

A wrong tile: DC 15 Dexterity save, 3d6 piercing, half on a success. An iron spike snaps up through the center of the tile.

The next room is wide and dark. Light catches a smooth vault door and a large brass spindle. The crank will not move until it is touched. Then the floor opens and a brass guardian rises in steam and attacks. When it dies, the spindle turns itself to the left, thuds, and the door creaks. The smell is old wood and metal.

The "room" is an alcove: a pedestal, two display cases, two chests. The chests are ordinary banded wood with no visible lock. The cases are fine wood with glass tops. The left case holds a lever-action firearm of gold metal and engraved ivory where the wood should be. The right case holds a staff: a brass bulb, an iron scepter, a decorated brass head hooked at one end, and a small brass cauldron beside the hook. That is the Staff of the Cauldron Carrier. The pedestal wears a miner's hat, a metal helm with a glass lamp on the front. The sheet calls it ordinary-looking. It is a helmet of identification, and it is the steel helmet with the monocle flip-downs Clive still wears in Ironspire. The plaque above it reads, in Dwarven, "Creators of the Orecrusher's Fortune."

The left chest: 1,600 sp, 80 gp, and a pouch of seven small gems at 50 gp each, plus a darkened silver ring cracked across an otherwise smooth face. That ring is the Ring of Seething, later in Tor's gear. The right chest is a mechanical mimic. It opens with a click, metal teeth, and surprise.

##### The Shattered Colossus

Player text, from Digs & Dives: the remains of a colossal golem, broken into massive stone fragments. Ancient symbols on every piece, believed to be Aetherian. Only the head is fully unearthed. Hollow, sightless eyes that some say glow faintly at night. Deep in the forest east of Steamwood.

**GM.** The head lies sideways in a shallow crater, moss and vines over most of it. A large hollow mouth. Two sockets, one eye still intact and faintly glowing. Through the mouth, the cranial chamber is a large empty room, a little buried. In a corner, under dirt and moss, a large metal-and-stone sphere hums faint blue.

Setting the eye in the socket clicks it home. The floor drops open at the back. A ladder, tilted with the head, goes down into the dark.

The chest cavity is vast. A massive crystal sphere hangs in the center, deep with ancient runes, pulsing slow red, like a fading heartbeat. A low hum in the floor. Jagged struts, glyphs, thick roots in the cracks. Rats dart at the edges. The red light catches their eyes.

The left arm is stale, cold, and nearly black. Rubble. The "rats" have eerie red eyes and follow. Spiky green creatures leap out and throw balls of flame. At the far end, a smooth stone door, sealed, etched with cold blue runes. Treasure here: three Aetherian canisters of liquid mana, each about three ounces full, and two Aetherian scrolls of magic circle keyed to fiends.

The right arm is warmer and smells of acrid smoke. Faint red and orange light from a stone camp. A rubble wall. On it, purple-skinned creatures with a short mass of spiky green tentacles at the chin. They shout backward. They sit around dim orange mushrooms, sharpening makeshift weapons and marking the walls. Treasure: a Soul Orb, an Infernal Fang Dagger, and a storage key. Both of those items are later in Clint's and Khan's gear. The key is not described anywhere else.

##### The Sandstorm Marauders

**GM.** The early shape of the Marauders, before Tor Longtooth unified them and before the attack on Dusthaven. A small band of desert raiders in unique masks. Zephyr Scorchblade is the tactical chieftain. Sirocco Emberweaver is the genius pyromancer. At a convenient moment, often while the party is in the mountains, they steal the party's mounts. They are gathering strength to rob a train coming from Ironspire to Dusthaven. Tracks in the sand lead to a limestone cliff cave, three rooms deep. Kentucky James, a tomb robber of this band, is already dead in the Sunfire Temple when the party reaches area 24. His Dusthaven bounty is still written open.

1. The entry. A couple of active marauders, one outside the mouth, side rooms used as sleeping quarters, and a single tent.
2. The meeting cavern. A crude fence holds the stolen mounts. Shoddy tables. A few marauders and a captain.
3. The planning room. The two leaders stand over a map of the coming heist. An arcane trap throws back anyone not wearing a marauder mask: DC 17 Strength, 4d6 force and 10 feet back on a failure, half and no push on a success.

##### Cult of Boogle

Ages ago, dwarves dug too deep and uncovered Boogle, a powerful vampire sealed in the mountains. His release destroyed that stretch of dwarven civilization except for the dwarves who kneeled. He wants blood and violence, and above all subservience. Exposure to him, and the long life underground, turned those dwarves into duergar. They are cannibals now. Sometimes they come to the surface to hunt. Below, their own housing is crude. The buildings they keep carving for Boogle are grand.

BBQ's pack includes raw duergar gold, ten pounds. The Boogle Ki Ring is in Gulp's gear.

##### Xanthis' Tower

**GM.** His actions in The Shadow are with that quest.

**Basement, the harbor.** The river bends and the tower looms. Flat ground after miles of ravine. Desiccated skeletons, mooring ropes, a long-broken dock. An arch into the bottom floor: crates, urns, and one huge zombie in a fine suit of splint, slowly checking crates. On the northeast wall a beam holds a bell and a thick rope. Ring the bell and the skeletons stand up to do what the harbormaster asks. The boxes are weapons, bows, and arrows.

**Floor 0, the undead organ.** A slow harrowing tune. A mountain of rotting muscle plays a giant organ with his back to the room. A skeleton on a central table. Dull green energy swells with the song. Coffins and loose skeletons along the walls. Crates of poor melee weapons, bows, and arrows. A giant arched double door west. At the climax the skeleton bolts up, grabs a rusty sword, and goes out to a battle. The door shuts. The ogre-zombie lays out another skeleton and starts again.

The ogre can, as an extra action, play a short melody and animate two skeletons from the piles. The closet has better arms: a +1 shortbow, a +1 shield, and a +1 chain shirt. On the table by the organ: a dark dagger with green gems in the handle. It deals necrotic instead of piercing. Used on a corpse, it rots the flesh off the bone where it is left. Also 40 gp, 280 sp, and a pot of 2,000 cp. A fresh humanoid skeleton lies on that edge of the table. An open crate beside it is full of rotten meat and organs.

**Floor 1, the laboratory.** Dim. Shelves of ingredients, bubbling potions, arcane apparatus. A large cauldron on a stone pedestal. Inscriptions and diagrams, clutter, embalmed cadavers of various creatures. A coffin-shaped double door on the south wall, closed and locked. Beside it a knocked-over potion. The last drop of a dark blue-gray liquid hits the boards among shattered glass.

The diagrams and ingredients are unlabeled. In order, they read as a huge flask of viscous red liquid, crushed clear crystals, a light fluffy ball floating in its bottle, a purple powder, red and orange flowers, and chunks of fermented root. In that order they make an elixir of strength, a potion of invisibility, a draught of levitation, an elixir of clairvoyance, and a potion of fire breath. The root reverses each: weakness, faerie fire, heaviness that forces the drinker prone, foggy mind, and cold body.

The door opens if someone drinks heaviness and cold body and falls backward into it. Their weight swings the door. A force catches them before the floor. Passing through the archway cancels the potions.

**Floor 4, maybe, the bedroom.** The sheet itself questions the floor number. The chest of magical experiments holds:

- **Boots of Gravity.** Bonus action to toggle. While on, the wearer is immune to forced movement, including being knocked prone, gains a climb speed equal to their walk, and all movement is halved.
- **Potion of Invincibility.** Bonus action to drink. For one hour the drinker no longer knows their hit-point total and feels no pain. The damage still happens.
- **Circlet of True Sight.** Bonus action. True sight out to a chosen range. Every 10 feet of range costs 2d6 psychic, and that damage cannot be reduced.
- **Gnarled twisted wooden wand.** A dull green glow in the roots. A magical charge inside moves with gravity. When it moves, the wand says "wahHH" or "WAHhh," higher as the charge nears the tip, and a little louder every time. An action spent shaking it makes it fully green: bright light 10 feet, dim light 10 feet beyond, for one minute.

Also dozens of gemstones and a large pile of gold and silver. The sheet says the party should feel they ought to reunite with Tor before looting any of it. The party receives 600 gp.

##### Aetheria and the Sunfire Prism

Aetheria rose in 303, three years after liquid mana was discovered, and fell in 686, three years after the Enigma Accident. The Sunfire Prism sheet says it was a mighty society that revered celestial powers and harnessed the sun. The ruins lie under the desert's shifting sands. Vaelrix Morra, Overseer of the Arcane, was born there more than six hundred years ago, which puts his birth around or before the fall.

The prism is said to be lost in those remains. Elowen Sunweaver created the temple that housed it, and her journals of the search are sealed in the tower that is now the Dusthaven mayor's office. One of those journals is the sheet Elowen's Journal. The weapon and the artifact entry are The Sun Prism, under Tor Longtooth's gear, because that is the folder the item sheet lives in.

##### The Sunfire Prism

**GM. Revealing the temple.** Inside a nest are eight marbles: blue, yellow, red, green, purple, orange, white, and black. The sheet says "6 marbles" and then lists eight colors. Under the remains of the nest, alone in the desert, a stone circle is carved with a triangle and an indent at each corner. Around the platform:

> To forge ahead, rewind the thread, Where the journey began, the path ahead is spread. In the footsteps of yore, your course is read, To reach your goal, return to where you were led.

Blue, green, and white, set in the triangle, raise a stone temple out of the desert. Sand pours off it like water. It blots out the sun. A long stair leads to a pair of stone doors.

##### Sunfire Temple

The sheet is a string of keyed areas and treasure, all GM notes. Areas that are not listed were not on the sheet.

**Area 7.** A stone platform in a natural cave. Another platform far opposite. Wooden disks hang from the ceiling on massive steel chains. The cave floor is a pool of boiling oil: viscous black bubbles, an unforgettable stench. A geyser erupts as the party enters, spraying oil and steam and shaking platforms. Every jump is a DC 15 Athletics check. A simple failure means the jumper stays put rather than fall. Failing by 5 or more leaves them dangling, with disadvantage on Strength saves if a geyser hits, and a DC 10 Athletics check to climb back. Failing by 10 or more is a fall. The oil deals 4d10 fire at the start of each turn spent in it.

**Area 8.** The door opens into utter blackness. Ctenmiir is a vampire here, cursed by an avatar of the sun to stay in a trance except when roused to defend his treasure. Under the coffin: the first ingredient, a glass orb glowing from within and full of constantly swirling sand. Also 5,000 sp, 1,500 gp, a potion of mind reading, and scrolls of summon elemental, dispel magic, and magic mouth.

**Area 10.** The kelpie's lair: 600 gp, the Amulet of Shifting Seaweed, and a +1 chain shirt.

**Area 13.** Snarla's sanctum: 250 gp and seven fine-cut gems at 200 gp each.

**Area 17.** Behind a brass chest, a pool of bubbling radiance. Harsh to look at. Treasure: a giant ruby worth 2,000 gp, four large gems at 500 gp each, Goggles of Night, and a luckstone.

**Area 24.** The hydra's lair holds the corpse of Kentucky James, a famous tomb robber turned marauder. His bounty is still open, dead or alive, for 1,000 gp. He also carries 150 gp, four 50 gp gems, and Boots of Striding.

**Area 27.** The second ingredient: a glass prism with a brass frame. Also 20 pp, 600 gp, four pieces of fine jewelry at 150 gp each, two potions of greater healing, and a scroll of protection against fiends. Qesnef wears a ring of protection, a ring of spell storing with two mirror images in it, and an Urnblade.

**Crafting the prism.** Insert the gel-orb into the prism and dip it in the Pool of Radiance. A blinding light, then a woman's voice. She congratulates whoever bested her trials, says they deserve the artifact they are about to recreate, and asks them to come to the Enigma and ask for Newole. She hopes to see them soon. The light goes. The pool is dimmer. Above it floats a prism of shifting desert colors, with a holy aura, comfortably warm. Then the room heats. The bubble fades. Steam comes through holes. The ground shakes, then quakes. A DC 14 Dexterity save or 3d8 bludgeoning from falling brick. Sand reaches the knees at the spiral stair. They outrun it to the entry and dive under the crumbling arch into harsh desert sun.

What meets them is a sharp toothy grin and the cocking of firearms, barely heard over the stone. Brown scales, reptilian eyes: Tor Longtooth. The grin becomes recognition, surprise, and sorrow.

He is grieved that fate reunited them here. Old comrades, separated by differences too vast to bridge. He names himself Tor Longtooth, Chieftain of the Marauders. This is the same Tor who keeps the tent in Highrest. He did not leave out of spite. He could not tolerate "that heathen," or parley with necromantic forces. He says it tore at his soul, and he would not watch the lands fall under that shadow. He tells the newer members they do not know the shared history, but that the party now stands in the way of his quest to reclaim the lands from the necromantic threat. Stand aside, or he is forced here and now.

He had followed them from Dusthaven in secret. Zephyr Scorchblade and Sirocco Emberweaver are already dead, killed when the party broke the Marauders' attack there and freed the captives. What he wants from this doorway is the Sun Prism the party has just remade.

He offers a hand, with real pity. Hand over the Sun Prism and come with him, as allies or as forced guests, only for a moment. He does not want pride to drive them further apart.

If it becomes a fight, he will not kill them. He chooses nonlethal. The sheet says the party cannot win this fight short of a miracle. He takes them to the marauders' fortress.

As allies, they see the war room: a death knight holding a castle to the north, maps of necromancer routes and patrols, a tally of guards and unique beings, a multitude of undead, signs of fiends.

As guests, he asks for weapons and magic for the time being and puts them in an iron cell in a stone prison inside the camp. He asks forgiveness, says there is an invasion to prepare, thanks them for reclaiming the relic, and promises to be back within an hour to talk further. Two guards overlook the cell.

He takes the Sun Prism. From this point the party works with him because he forces it. He makes them lead the way into the mountains north of Dusthaven, toward Castle Darkstone. They clear that castle of Aric. Paul Marauder dies there and is buried in the town that follows, Highrest. The fortress is the band's camp after Tor unified them, not the three-room cave Zephyr and Sirocco used. The war-room map's death knight is not given a name on the sheet. The castle they then clear is Castle Darkstone, and its master is Aric.

##### The Underdark

##### Myconid Colony

Deep under the mountains, a sealed country with very few entrances. Things here grew without sunlight and without much to eat. Flora dominates: glowing mushrooms, mystic plants, strange fungi. A handful of dangerous fauna dominate their own ground. Legends talk about long-lost dwarves, and about a species of especially psychedelic mushroom.

The tribe, as the sheet lists them: Elder Mycelor, the Shroomcaller, Moldmangler, Sporebreaker, Sporecap, Mossfoot, Fungulus, Mycelius, Moldspore, Mosswhisper.

A false prophet has risen in the darkest corners. Kyuss, pronounced Kee-OOS, the Worm that Walks. He takes the bodies of enemies and of those who give themselves, by a parasitic infection. The worms get under the flesh and drive the host. The myconids thought they were safe. Their bodies lack the usual places for the worms, and they could reject them. Kyuss grew stronger. The worms now attack myconid flesh directly, tearing them apart alive and driving the infected mad, craving only death.

He is deeper in the dark, and his forces are spread through the Underdark. The tribe knows an object of power, deeper even than where Kyuss lies. He knows of it too. His spawn fill the dark that has to be waded to reach it. They ask the explorers to help retrieve it, and offer whatever help they can give in return. The object is not named on this sheet. Sporosylvan, in Khan's gear, is a rare Underdark fungus with a healing radius, and it is the only object these sheets describe that way.

##### Altar of Rot

Waist-deep murky gray water. A few floating patches of purple fungus light the room. In the center, a statue of a robed humanoid with no face. The body looks composed of long strands gathered into limbs. It holds a stone dish with an extremely shallow layer of dirt. East, a large arch and closed stone doors. Something is written over the arch in a strange runic script.

In Undercommon, the outside reads as a warning to return the way you came. It also reads Altar of Rot.

Anything set in the dish rots fast, becomes dirt, and filters into the statue. Anything living that touches the plate takes 7 (2d6) necrotic damage. Enough dirt and the hands lower, a single Worm of Kyuss shoots out, and the doors open.

Scratched on the wall, almost looking like natural stone, in Thieves' Cant: "Starving, durt hurts, stupid worms." The spelling "durt" is the sheet's.

Under the scratches, at the bottom of the water, a partly decomposed halfling. His pack has thieves' tools, a couple of small gems at 10 gp, a dozen old unidentifiable silver coins, and a dagger. An Investigation check finds a pair of Boots of Elvenkind buried in the muck. Those boots are later filed under Khan.

##### The Ninth Circle (Treachery)

**GM, the cosmology.** A giant frozen lake. An empty icy wasteland as far as anyone can see, and stinging snowy wind that cuts that distance down. Inside the ice are the souls of people who betrayed those closest to them, forever reaching for whatever they sinned to get: wealth, women, or power. Beasts and devils roam, and they betray each other for scraps. Deep under the ice is the center of Hell, and Satan. Velkhana reigns over the stretch of ice where Krampus's portal came in. The Sack of Cold job out of Ironspire ends with Velkhana dead and one of two Velkhana crystals in the party's hands.

Three encounters are written apart from the scenes: succubi drift on the Ethereal Plane and wait for lost souls to sleep, so they can charm them and betray them. An ice devil patrols with a chained yeti, looking for defiant souls and rowdy beasts to press into the ice. A pair of frost giants wander, hunting. Lost souls look like enough of a meal.

**Arrival.** The party crawls through a canvas sack. It tightens until it feels as if it is squeezing the breath out. The air gets colder. Each breath hangs as mist too long. Then the sack drops away.

A world of blinding white. A massive frozen lake under a pale light with no source. Just under the ice, bodies in grotesque contortions, hands out. Each one reaches for something just past the fingers: gold, the outline of a lover, an intangible crown. Mouths frozen mid-scream. A frigid wind, and the faint venomous whispers of the damned. Somewhere, chains drag. Now and then a wail that is colder than the frost.

On the horizon, a jagged mountainous spire of glacial ice. Looking at it puts a dark pressure on the heart. It pulls.

Behind the party, a small faintly glowing portal hangs just over the ice, edges like disturbed water. Through it, the dim glow of the church. The portal already looks impossibly small, as if the sack is trying to close it, and the glow fades by the second.

A shadow sweeps the lake. The light dims. The cold bites harder. The ground trembles. A massive winged creature of ice, wings blotting out what is above, snow falling off every beat. A low growl in the chest. Glowing soulless eyes sweep the ice, hunting. Then it is gone over the horizon. This is the sighting. The sheet does not name it in the scene. The GM note names Velkhana as the ruler of this stretch.

**The giants.** Two massive shapes. Frost-covered skin, snow-white beards, tattered cloaks of frozen hides, pale blue eyes. Between them, a makeshift lantern of rock and bone. Inside, a crystal gives a faint golden warmth. One speaks, mournful, like distant thunder: they bear warmth where none exists. The giants want a gift, a memory of warmth, love, or joy, to feed the light. Without it the cold claims the giants. The smaller one adds that if the party refuses, the giants must survive, and the party's warmth will do. The lantern flickers. Desperation and regret.

The same bargain is the mechanic of the Shard of Warmth in Az's gear: speak a warm memory into the crystal, lose that memory, charge the shard.

**The traitor under the ice.** The wind carries sorrowful whispers. Cracks draw the eye down. A body just under the surface, hand up. The eyes move. The mouth moves with no sound. Then, in the mind: walk free while he stays bound, and he can help. Free him and he gives wealth, power, knowledge. All he asks is mercy.

If freed, the body claws up, almost liquefying to squeeze through, breaks out, looks at the sky, breathes, then flies at the rescuer and jumps inside. A Wisdom save or 3d6 psychic. The mind goes numb. New memories: a knife in an unsuspecting back, poison in a celebratory drink, adultery behind a close friend. "Learn from my mistakes, know the signs. I thank you." The boon is advantage on Insight checks to detect deceit, and a grown want for wealth. The sheet says "you expect more, however that may be achieved." Remove Curse can take the boon off.

If denied, he curses the party to belong in the ice with the rest. More figures stir under the ice, clutching bloodied gold, stolen hearts, broken blades. Together they ask, inside the mind: "Who among you can be trusted?" The effect is constant suspicion of anyone close, an inability to trust, and disadvantage on Wisdom saves against being charmed, until Remove Curse.

**The stair.** At the base of the spire, a giant crack in the spire and the ice makes a wide rough staircase into the solid lake. Not a long trip down. The stairs end at a short cavern. Forward is a wall of fog and snow.

**The beast.** Stepping out of the cave, the fog is gone. The howling wind is gone. An eerie silence. The ice feels fragile. Cracks spider from where the party stands. For the first time the frozen hell is visible: mountains of jagged ice like shattered teeth, glaciers glinting. For Khan, the landscape is not the thing that stops the breath. Miles away, something that seemed to be a hill of frost rises. Its shape is barely beastlike, and it locks eyes. Daggers of ice strike the heart, the stomach drops, and it is time to leave.

**Back to the portal.** Farther from the spire, the ice weakens. Large cracks. Desiccated souls try to crawl through. Steps start to splash in shallow puddles. The entry portal is a small circle of warm light over a chunk of ice still standing in the shallows. Souls clutch from the water, pull themselves up, hold each other down to use the bodies as leverage. Some who got out grapple each other. Some float toward the pale light. A pair see a metal glint. Hollow sockets full of greed. They clamber over each other toward the party.

**Sleep.** A succubus. Wisdom save. Hard to sleep. Nightmares of greed, of friends cutting a throat while the dreamer lies there. Then awake, still in the icy cave, standing, weapons ready. An incomprehensible beast towers in front of the dreamer. The party is at their side, and Val. Val shouts to get ready, says she has their backs, as always, holds up her cross, and prays. Divine magic swirls, lifts her habit, and the dream lingers on her. Val is not Queen Beatrice, and she is not Valentina. Valentina is the illusion in Aric's bed. This Val wears a habit and a cross.
