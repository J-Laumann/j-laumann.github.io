#### Anchorfall

A bustling port. In this small, busy town, money buys literally anything. Ships move through the delta that joins the town to the sea, carrying cargo from across the land. The constant commerce moves cargo between boats and trains. Steam works never stop. Shipfaced, The Admiral's Table, Portside Provisions, The Kraken's Cache, the church, the stables, and the docks. From Brews to Bullion adds The Staggering Steer and Moorwind Magics, neighbors, joined through the basement after the wine disaster. This port's priest is Father Kirk Callidus. The docks' rates are below. Anchorfall is not on the railway.

##### Shipfaced

The local tavern. Food: Salty Seaweed Chips 5 cp. Salted Fish Jerky 2 sp. Grilled Oysters, with a tangy vinegar sauce and a hint of garlic, 3 sp. Spiced Mussels, steamed with hearty spices and herbs, 5 sp. Creamy Clam Chowder, with clams and potatoes, 4 cp. Fisherman's Feast, grilled fish fillets, crab cakes, and roasted root vegetables, 8 sp. Turf Roast, roast beef with gravy and mashed potatoes, 8 sp. The Dockworker's Platter, smoked fish, sausages, and pickled vegetables, 3 sp.

Drink: Ale of the Bay 5 cp. Spiced Grog 1 sp. Smoky Cider 2 sp. Stormbringer Stout 3 sp. Ask about the catch of the day.

**GM.** Mira Stonebrew runs it, a tough and lively dwarf with a reputation for outdrinking the heartiest sailors. Booming laugh, quick temper, and endless stories from her younger days as a Steamwood miner and then a brewer. She takes in drifters and wayward sailors, and she offers them work. The room runs from drinking contests to sea-shanty sing-alongs. Stormbringer Stout is her own brew, said to knock out seasoned sailors. She keeps the place lively and safe.

##### Portside Provisions

Sells adventuring gear. Ordinary goods use the prices in [Mundane goods](#mundane-goods).

**GM.** Elara "Silverhand" Gale, a retired sailor. She lost her left hand to a pirate attack and replaced it with a silver prosthetic. Decades at sea gave her an eye for goods and a knack for bartering. Gruff, fair, sharp, and well liked. The shop is meticulously organized, from basic provisions to rare maritime trinkets, and she will share a tale or advise a sailor passing through.

##### The Kraken's Cache

Sells unique objects and magical items. No prices are written.

**GM.** Merrick Deepwater, a tall, lean half-elf. Salt-and-pepper hair, piercing sea-green eyes, weathered. He was navigator on a cursed ship, learned arcane secrets and forbidden knowledge, and narrowly escaped a pact with a kraken. He left the sailor's life and opened the shop to trade what he had collected. Many of the pieces are rumored to have come from the ocean's depths or from otherworldly dealings. He is intimidating and approachable, and his advice is cryptic and useful. Locals whisper that he still holds a bond with the kraken, and that crossing him risks the wrath of the deep.

##### The Docks

First class is 1 gp per 10 miles. Second class is 2 sp per 10 miles.

##### The Staggering Steer

Taurus Moorwind owns it, lives here, and brews. Enoki Chanterelle works under him. She is the moon druid and barbarian in the From Brews to Bullion party, and the two of them are dedicated to each other. Gretchen Moorwind, his old black cat, lives here too. Moorwind Magics is the next building. After the wine disaster the two connect through the basement. The hearth, the bar, and the cellar door are the room that was played.

At the highest point of the hearth, on the stone chimney, a giant pair of bull horns is mounted. They have a slight twist and a bone-and-black patterning. Below them, four pewter goblets sit on the mantle, each with a clear gem fitted into the front. Below the goblets, built into the bricks, a wooden placard with silver trimmings says nothing. Further down, the remains of the fire still glow, embers mixed with fallen bricks and dust.

The silver trimmings have a small spherical indent in the lower middle. Splashed with alcohol, they reveal: "Raise the tide in the shallow shell and the next of my whispers I shall tell." Until the marble is set in that indent, the goblets are held to the hearth magnetically and are nearly impossible to yank off.

Behind the bar is a wooden chest with a lock on the front and a small slit in the top. A row of cupboards is built at floor level. Shelves of alcohol sit on them, and the quality rises as the shelves do. In the middle of the cupboards, half of an open barrel has been made into a drain tub.

The tub has a small tube in the front right, and something shimmers down in it. If the pipes underneath are closed and the tub fills, a hollow crystal marble floats up. A small chain drags a silver key behind it.

Under the cupboards are a pair of copper pipes from the tub, and washcloths, soaps, and other cleaning supplies. Hidden among the supplies is a corked vial of crushed red crystals with a dull glow. These are Greater Smelling Salts, 4d4+4.

The locked chest holds 23 gp and 83 sp in a drop box under the slot. A compartment to the left holds six fine gold-trimmed glass flutes and a single gold ring carved with nature motifs. That ring is a Ring of Animal Influence.

The marble goes into the indent on the mantle. The placard clicks and rotates:

> Four cups to fill, four cups to drain.
> Each a story in the Moorwind vein.
> When emptied and placed on the embered stone,
> The key to the cask shall be your own.

A spell cast while holding one of the clear-gem goblets is absorbed. The gem turns a color for the school, and for the damage type if the spell has one. The colors keep shifting until the drink in that goblet is drunk. Then they stop, and the goblet may go back on the hearth.

| School | Color |
| --- | --- |
| Abjuration | Blue |
| Conjuration | Violet |
| Divination | Glittering white |
| Enchantment | Rose gold |
| Evocation | Burning red |
| Illusion | Wispy pink |
| Necromancy | Sickly green |
| Transmutation | Silver and gold |

When each finished drink has been set back, the mantle rotates again and reveals a small purple crystal on a short keychain. Reaching toward the cellar door while holding that crystal lifts the door open to meet the hand. It moves with no resistance.

##### Moorwind Magics

The shop next door to The Staggering Steer, in Anchorfall. Thaddeus Moorwind owns it. No stock list is written. He will sell the From Brews to Bullion party magical objects at a discount. The amount of the discount is not given. After the wine disaster the basement connects this shop to the tavern.

#### Umimura

A coastal city on the eastern edge of the Savagebloom Expanse.

**GM.** A coastal town in every sense. Salt in the nose. The best food you have ever smelled, coming out of stalls. Fishers and sailors. The sea is treated as endless, and the town takes all of its living from it. Sandstone buildings, walls covered in murals.

Umimura is a heavily eastern-inspired city. Tradition, family, and nobility organize it. Opioids and sex tourism are a large part of what the town is for.

#### Evershroud

An island in a fog that does not cross to the mainland. The fog was always there, a light magical mist off an extremely powerful Node of Air near the center of the main island, and it shaped the plants and the animals. Aetherian magitech workers studied it. The Lady of the House led that work until the Enigma Project took her, and the research house stood empty through the Age of Terror. After the Enigma Accident she came back, around the year 700, half-attached to her soul and an arch-fey, and she took the house by the node.

In 970 the fog thickened fast. The things in it grew aggressive. Breathing it began to cause Mad Mist disease. People left in droves. Carter Wint came in 974, planted a barrier in the church, and left an Aurelic Crystal. The present company reaches the island on summer 23–24, meets the people, and goes into the fog.

##### Crazy Fog Man

Pockets of Fog Fever can infect a person. Watch for hues of blue. It drives them mad. Mister gave Kayren a scarf he made. Everyone but Borin was infected with Mad Mist.

**GM.** The only GM line is a link, `https://imgur.com/xVNgUMp`, labeled Mad Mist. The picture is not copied into this book.

##### Mad Mist Effects

Kayren feels he must repeat small acts three times: washing his hands, counting coins, touching things. Kincaid hallucinates, vividly, and has disadvantage on ability checks because of it.

##### Fogbell Tower

The lighthouse was turned to a new job as the fog got heavier. It holds a giant brass bell, rung when ships are coming in, and heard for miles. Sailors say it rings on its own, just before a wreck.

##### The Church

This is Evershroud's church, not the imperial list. The player side is empty.

**GM.** Father Samuel is sure the fog is cursed for a greater reason, and he means to lift it. He is onto a triangulated source. Too many days, the fog pours from deep in the island, and it never reaches the mainland, so the source is here. Carter's barrier from 974 is in this building.

##### The Blind Gull

**GM.** Worn stone and salt-stained wood, leaned just above the docks, the biggest building in town besides the church. The sign creaks on rusted chains: a flying seagull, clouds over its eyes, and the name The Blind Gull. A hurdy-gurdy covers the door. Amber lanterns, more than a dozen round tables, a bar at the back, and fog inside that is not the town's fog. It smells of sweet tobacco. Most tables have at least one pipe. Mara watches the company from behind the counter. No one else looks up. Rooms are 1 sp a night. Food and drink, if they like fish and cheap ale.

##### Grandma's Pearlob Pie

Little Donny's meemaw Edith used to make a pear pie from those same pears. She had a secret grove when she was younger. She has not gone since the fog thickened. She will tell them where, for a barrel of Pearlobs.

##### Evershroud Manor

The sheet itself has one room.

**GM.** The foyer is huge. Dozens of coats on a pair of racks. A giant olive rug, low sconces, warmer and more colored than the fog outside. A couch and two dusty side tables. Past them, to the left, a stair goes up.

An animated oak stands in front of the manor. The only proof is the Bark of Debarking, which can force a Transport via Plants to that tree.

##### Dinner

The seating, before the night turns. Kincaid and Borin have salad. Winston and Kayren have soup. Kincaid has pork. Everyone else has beef.

##### Party Night

The company is asleep in silken beds. The dreams sour. Family and friends in familiar rooms go wrong. Windows show nothing. Voices change pitch. Fog comes in until the dream is dull gray. A laugh comes from everywhere, and they wake. The head pounds. The voice is gone. A click, and the door opens a crack. Something passes. Winston screams for help, a man's voice, and the door slams.

Firelight. A standing mirror that was not there before. The reflection wears clothes too elegant and too brightly colored. Those clothes are in the closet. They fit with no room to spare, silk, large pockets, thickly padded. Armor cannot be worn under them. They grant +1 AC. If the wearer does not look exactly like the mirror, the door does not open.

**Upstairs hallway.** Two staircases. Fog covers them from the landing down, and the lower floor is the same fog. Nothing shows through it.

**Armor hallway.** The wide hall north has four suits of armor, empty hands at their sides. The carpet's pattern will not sit still. Every door tried is locked. The most perceptive sees the pattern spell, one word at a time: "Demonstrate. The. Rules." One member has a note in a pocket that should explain them. The armor has to be posed as hear no evil, speak no evil, see no evil, and "Have fun!" A pair of doors then clicks. They are the two doors at the south end, into the smoking lounge and the pleasure room.

**Smoking lounge.** Two chairs, a table, a shelf of lit candles, a long carved pipe. The south walls are almost all leaded glass, and outside is fog. Dozens of hands come out of that fog and beat on the glass. One candle goes out. A string of smoke sinks into the shadows on the wall.

**Pleasure room.** Just say yes, and move on when finished.

**Trophy room.** Hides on the walls. Several jump up and are rugs of smothering. When that is done, the bathroom has the loot, and a door appears in the trophy room while no one is looking.

**Winston's room.** They open it in time to see pixies sewing up a hole in the bed that is exactly Winston's shape.

**Dining room.** The room they ate in, lower light, a chandelier, fog on the windows. A meal at each head of the table. Dead pixies on the floor, still softly glowing, and a pair of small golden scissors with cut threads. Pockets can be cut open. Inside: a scroll of ray of frost, the note "The drinks will be ready in a weeks time," the note "Mind. Blown. If you can comprehend the flavors it's worth it. 2 out of 5 stars," a dish of steaming salad and nearly magmatic pork, and a solid soup beside dead-cold beef. A fire spell and a cold spell on the dishes swing the doors open, into the hallway and the kitchen.

**Pantry.** A vaguely humanoid figure of glittering stardust, the pattern of the sky staying put as the body moves on to a new patch of it. A cauldron under one arm, an extra limb stirring. It ignores them until it talks. The voice is low and beautiful, and it echoes. It cannot find the last ingredients.

"Wielded by wit, not by hand, yet is often held?" The sheet's answer is tongue.

"I am kin to thought, yet cannot be controlled. I am spawned in darkness, and banished by dawn." The sheet's answer is dream.

"I am invisible, yet all know my sharpness. From nothingness I come, the enemy of the strongest army." The sheet does not give this answer. The cook treats it as solved anyway.

He offers a spoon of the sludge. The color cannot be described, and the smell cannot be named. It floods the mind. Roll a d6. Take −1 to Wisdom, Intelligence, or Charisma. Gain the fey-touched feat without the +1. He laughs, says that was far too much for a mortal, and digs out a large twinkling pearl whose pattern turns slowly.

**Downstairs hallway.** The sheet numbers this 9 as well. The fog is gone. A table sticks sideways, at an angle, out of a circular rug and into the floor. A light held near the sundial rug and walked around it, as if seven days were passing, unlocks the cellar.

**Tea room.** A satyr woman giggles, chin in her hands, at a satyr man leaned back and grinning. He asks for drinks. He tips well. He is getting lucky. He wants a cup taken to his brother in the front lounge, who may be angry that his turn was stolen. A drink should settle him. The coin he flicks is silver, trapezoidal, stamped with a crown.

**Cellar.** Large barrels, one tapped, several smaller glasses, and about a dozen silver goblets and tankards.

**Lounge.** The purple lounge from before they went up. Firelight, and a small man with long pointed ears, messy orange hair under a tall top hat. Playing cards stick out of his suit and out of the hatband. He says the room is taken. He is waiting on a girl. Then he is sick of "foids" and of "chads," says he can smell it on the company, and flicks his wrist. Cards pour from his sleeves, turn point-first, and initiative is rolled. He shouts that this is not the last the world will see of him, that Emberheim has fallen, that millions must die. He pops away. A small silver key hits the floor. By the couch: thick leather gloves, and a card scribbled with bad pickup lines.

**Bedroom.** Rowdy music, laughing, a room packed past its size. Gnomes with elves, satyrs stepping around rabbit folk and fox folk, bugbears reaching over the crowd, a centaur against the wall with a tankard. Winston is on a bed in a clearer corner. Alistaire and Malistaire sit with him. They say the friend is alive because they made sure of it.

Winston's bugbear line runs back to the explosion of magic at the Enigma. This mist is that magic mixed with a powerful Node of Air. He was breathing it in. So were many of the company. In his blood it is stronger. He is falling into the mind of the fae. He writhes. His fur bubbles. Alistaire and Malistaire know of nothing to stop it, not even the Lady. They can ease the transition. The company takes that offer. He becomes a full fae. The large group of fae leaving the manor, rowdy on the way out, whisks him back to the Enigma.

**The Lady of the House.** If asked, Alistaire and Malistaire lead them across the hall. She thanks the pair. They hang in the praise, then shut the door.

She has not had guests of this sort in years. She is surprised Evershroud still exists. She thought the fog would have emptied it. Her last visitor was Carter Wint. He took a night like this one and was never charmed by it. She wishes he had stayed. A Node of Air sits just north of the manor. She studies it, and she is interested in what the fae do to it. The fog is the obvious effect. Carter came for the same reason, went through the node, and never returned. The beast weakening the node is the Auran golem, made by the Aarakocra from Carter's old notes. The handiwork is close to his. The company destroys it. She is in their debt for that.

"Mind. Blown. If you can comprehend the flavors it's worth it. 2 out of 5 stars."

"The drinks will be ready in a weeks time."

"Just Say Yes."

"Listen to the wise monkeys. And above all, have fun! It would be terribly rude not to."

The first two are also inside the cut pockets at the dining table. "Just Say Yes" is the pleasure room. The monkeys are the armor in the hall.
