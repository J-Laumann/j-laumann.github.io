## Locations

Emberheim in 980. Towns first, then the other names printed on the map, then the sites that are neither.

### Emberheim

The map is the empire in the present year. Umimura was Tidewatch. Anchorfall was Mariner's Rest.

#### The years

The Empire of Emberheim rules from Ironspire. Its banner is a compass rose set before a roaring flame, with a fanciful E in the center. The empire's business is invention. Condensed mana, liquid mana, firearms, and clockwork sit beside churches, bounty boards, and desert towns that still run on horses and bad whiskey.

Liquid mana was discovered in **300**. It sells at **50 gp an ounce**. The capital runs on condensed mana. Dusthaven grew up around an oasis of the same stuff in the western desert. Aetheria, an older sun-worshipping civilization, rose in **303** and fell in **686**.

#### Ironspire

Ironspire is the capital: constant invention, metal, and mana. The Empire protects its citizens here with breakthroughs in condensed mana. Magitech inventors run through a city that seems made of metal and steam. Emberheim exports creations and imports raw materials. It is a hungry town.

Most buildings are metallic spears shoved up through the steam that clings to the streets like fog. None of them may rise above the Imperial Palace. The Council meets there to pass laws, order the military, and approve new inventions.

The stolen schematic, the gigs, the sack of cold, the contest, and the audience are in Weird West.

##### Imperial Palace

The center of government. The Council of Emberheim passes the major laws. Seven chairs:

| Seat | Charge |
| --- | --- |
| Scales | Overseer of Trade |
| Ship | Overseer of Transportation |
| Open Book | Overseer of the Arcane |
| Gear | Overseer of Innovation |
| Tree | Overseer of Land |
| Cross | Overseer of Faith |
| Swords | Overseer of War |

**GM, from Official Business.** A Bronze Hero Badge hums and shocks in the pocket when Torren Blackholt calls. He waits at the palace doors, hands behind his back, and says the empire has a small request, and that a couple of church figures wanted to catch the heroes while they were in the building.

The lobby is about thirty feet high. A thin waterfall glosses a brass map of Emberheim on the left. Another waterfall covers seven brass icons, the council seats. Stone and brass run to stairs. Guards on the ground floor ignore Torren and the party. A gnome in religious garb waves. Saint Nicholas stands beside him.

Nicholas thanks them for the church, congratulates them on becoming heroes, and says he knew they could do it. The gnome thanks them for the people they saved and for what the church got out of the Velkhana crystal. He returns the crystal. The research confirms they entered the Ninth Circle of Hell and fought one of its guardians. The notes themselves will not help much. He wants someone capable to look at the crystal: it is a serious source of magical power and could make fantastic gear. Nicholas adds that the badges make it much easier to convince the church to hire them, then leaves.

Torren takes them up in a brass cage. Levers and a button. Several floors up he opens double doors and announces the young heroes. Six figures sit on slightly raised podiums. The sheet ends there. Faith's seat is the empty one: Archbishop Evalyne is marked Missing, which leaves six councilors in the room.

##### The overseers

**Trade, Orwin Delvane.** A portly man in his mid-fifties, neatly trimmed goatee, robe lined with gold thread. He always smells faintly of cologne and ink. His rings jingle when he signs paperwork. Sheet: Trade - Orwin Delvane.

**Transportation, Essira Tidewake.** A lean water genasi woman with light-blue skin and long white hair in a single braid. She wears a weather-beaten naval coat pinned with compasses. She leans back in her chair and would put her muddy boots on the table if anyone allowed it. Sheet: Transportation - Essira Tidewake. The Flying Boats sheet Essira Tidewake repeats this description and adds nothing.

**Arcane, Vaelrix Morra.** An ageless, regal elven man with luminous violet eyes and layered robes inscribed with glyphs. His voice carries an aged weight. His left hand stays in a silk glove and the glove never comes off. **GM.** He was born in Aetheria more than six hundred years ago. Sheet: Arcane - Vaelrix Morra.

**Innovation, Fenwick Sprox.** A middle-aged gnome. Blond hair slicked back with streaks of silver, silver goggles over a blue left eye and a brown right eye. A mechanical arm rests on the table under a stained leather coat. Sheet: Innovation - Fenwick Sprox.

**Land, Bren Tallwood.** He looks ancient: towering, calm, moss in the beard, bark-like skin along the arms. A long green cloak clasped with antler bone. He speaks slowly, as if weighing every word like stone. Sheet: Land - Bren Tallwood.

**Faith, Archbishop Evalyne.** The player note is one word: Missing. Sheet: Faith - Archbishop Evalyne.

**War, Kael Durnhart.** A broad-shouldered dwarf in ceremonial steel even in council. His beard is braided into three points tipped with iron clasps. His voice is gravel on steel. A sword leans on every table he sits at. Sheet: War - Kael Durnhart.

##### Brass & Barter

Sells adventuring gear, food and drink, tools, blacksmithing, medicine, and potions. Ordinary gear, tools, and trade goods use the prices in [Mundane goods](#mundane-goods).

Steam hisses when the door opens. Clockwork hums. The place smells of polished metal and oiled leather. Dark wood shelves with brass fittings hold rations and gadgets. A brass chandelier turns overhead. Automaton assistants click between the aisles. The counter shows high-end clockwork, with a sturdy brass scale for supplies and haggling. Behind it, a wall of gears carries the mark: a cogwheel twisted with a coin.

##### The Grease Trap

**GM.** Under the streets, down a slick iron stair, the air is sweet smoke and spilled ale. The underground room mixes low talk, glass, and the occasional fist. Soot, oil, a sticky floor, furniture that looks salvaged from scrap: mismatched tables, dented mugs, a bar held together with rivets and glue. Ruffians dice in the corner. Another table plans a score. Tobacco drifts from an alcove where a cloaked figure watches.

##### Briggs' BOOM!

Sells firearms, dynamite, ammunition, and accessories. Experimental ammunition, in compact, medium, or shell, is 25 gp. Long experimental rounds are 30 gp. The named experimental loads are the Heart Seeker Bullet, the Flak Shell, and the Lightning Rod Bullet. Their rules are in the arms chapter.

**GM.** Gunpowder, oil, and hot metal. The room is a chaos of parts, tools, and guns under warm lamps. Behind a cluttered counter, a gnome with wild gray hair squints at a pistol and talks to himself. His mechanical arm clicks, screwdriver-hand on a bolt. Something behind the counter thumps like a clock. Muffled gunfire comes through the wall and he ignores it. A sign says "Test Before You Buy" and points left. His line: "If it's got a trigger, we've got it, or I'll build it." The named sheet gives him the full name Briggs Magren. The sheet titled Briggs has no other text.

##### Crystals and Curios

**GM.** A bell, then old parchment, polished brass, and something arcane. Enchanted gadgets hum on cramped shelves. Gears and oddities cover the walls in an organized chaos. Clive Orecrusher sits on a worn stool behind the counter, beard grayer than when the party last saw him in Scorpion Gulch. He still wears the steel mining helmet with the monocle flip-downs, the helmet of identification from his family's hall. He buys and sells magic items here because that helmet makes identification easy. He looks up from a mechanical bird: "Well, look who finally wandered over. What brings you lot to my little corner of Ironspire?"

##### The Gilded Gear

Rooms, per day: basic 8 sp, fancy 2 gp, penthouse 7 gp. All-inclusive: basic 2 gp, fancy 4 gp, penthouse 10 gp.

**GM.** Warm light, money, exotic incense, expensive liquor. A vast room of talk. Decorative brass gears turn in the walls and ceiling. Polished granite is inlaid with gold. Card tables, roulette, dice pits, an automaton pianist, bartenders in waistcoats with jewel-colored drinks. Balconies like opera boxes, lanterns, inn rooms behind frosted glass. High-roller rooms sit behind doorways of shrouded fog, several floors up. At the door, a humming bronze security arch ripples like liquid metal, and a uniformed attendant nods. Several floors up, Sorrin Quill leans on a railing and waves a black-feathered hand. See his entry under People.

##### Digs & Dives

The office's notable board lists three sites, each written up on its own sheet:

- The Shattered Colossus, in the forest east of Steamwood.
- The Isle of Falling Stars, in the Azure Lagoon.
- Scraps of Disaster, just outside the Enigma.

The office sheet itself has no further GM text.

#### Mundane

A smaller town on the central continent, north of Ironspire, in wooded hills that are just beginning to turn cold. A small sea lies between it and the Enigma. The map does not print the name. It is where people go to hone traditional magic, and the railway north ends beyond it at Glacier's Edge, about 125 miles, a day at 12 hours 30 minutes, 10 gp.

##### The College of Mundane

The school of the town. Anybody in the world may come looking for knowledge of magics. No masters, tuition, or library list are written.

#### Dusthaven

**GM.** Elowen Sunweaver founded Dusthaven in 906. She is an elven archmage of fire and sun, now a lich, living deep in the Enigma. The town does not know she founded it, does not know where she is, and does not know she is a lich. Those three facts have always been kept secret. Her tower is now the mayor's office, and her sealed journals are still in it. See People and Aetheria.

What Dusthaven tells is the town sheet's story: decades ago a human explorer found an oasis of condensed mana in the far west of Emberheim, in an unforgiving desert, and the town exploded out of it. The place is rough, full of beasts and bandits, and famous for bounty hunting. The Sheriff's Office posts outlaws, rogue magitech inventors, and the irregular creatures that threaten the town.

The Weird West company now knows more than the town does, and less than the truth. They know Elowen created the temple that housed the Sun Prism, and that she is a powerful wizard. They do not know she founded the town, where she is, or that she is a lich. The current mayor is Thorian Brightshield.

Named locations: Sheriff's Office, Gunther's Guns, Spare-a-drop Supplies, the Lounging Lizard Inn, the railway station, the stables, and the church.

The Marauder war, in order, is in Weird West. The cave they used while Zephyr and Sirocco led is in Miscellaneous locations.

##### Sheriff's Office

**GM.** Double doors, clinking metal, the hum of arcane machinery, a whiff of gunpowder. A stern deputy in a duster and a ten-gallon hat shuffles wanted posters at the central desk. Behind him an ornate clock shows floating, glowing roman numerals, and a self-playing piano fills the room. Posters of outlaws and fugitive magicians glow with runes that mark the bounties. A frosted-glass wall hides an imposing figure in a high-backed leather chair: the Sheriff of Dusthaven.

His office holds a large mahogany desk, a high-backed leather chair that hovers, and a massive ornate safe under complex wards. On the party's first return the deputy is lazy about it: "Oh hey, y'all are back? Board's over there, pick red, you're dead. Tear one off and good luck."

##### Sheriff of Dusthaven

The fog was always there, a light magical mist off an extremely powerful Node of Air near the center of the main island, and it shaped the plants and the animals. Aetherian magitech workers studied it. The Lady of the House led that work until the Enigma Project took her, and the research house stood empty through the Age of Terror. After the Enigma Accident she came back, around the year 700, half-attached to her soul and an arch-fey, and she took the house by the node.

In 970 the fog thickened fast. The things in it grew aggressive. Breathing it began to cause Mad Mist disease. People left in droves. Carter Wint came in 974, planted a barrier in the church, and left an Aurelic Crystal. The present company reaches the island on summer 23–24, meets the people, and goes into the fog.

##### Putting Beatrice to Rest

A hushed tomb. Tall stained glass on the east walls. Dim light on two coffins on white marble. The north coffin is marked King Krieg von Darkstone. The south coffin is marked "Queen Bea" and the rest is scratched out. The vaulted ceiling, thirty feet up, is an inlaid gold mosaic. A tall ethereal man stands with his hands behind his back, looking down and out through the glass. "It's beautiful, isn't it. The people finally return to village Darkstone." He turns: an aged, full face, a small smile.

His coffin holds a hollow wax replica of him at death, eyes closed, uncannily real. His wife's coffin holds a tattered white shroud and traces of dust.

He is glad to see the village alive again, and glad his son's torturous existence is over. He thanks the party if they admit they ended it. He asks one more thing. Aric killed his wife in a rage. Aric had always despised her for not being his birth mother. Her body is beneath her resting place, behind the struck-out plaque. Krieg wants the bones placed properly in the casket, and the plaque repaired if they can, so her soul can go with his. He has promised to wait for eternity. He warns that she has had a long time to grow angry in that small compartment, and that the party may do what they must, including striking her ghastly figure down.

If they do as asked, Beatrice reforms, unmarred, says nothing, and takes Krieg's hand. They look back. He tells the party to look within his heart for a proper reward, thanks them, and asks that this land never fall into corruption again. He adds that he is afraid the soul of his granddaughter still walks, twisted by corruption, and to look out for her. Then the two of them fade through the stained glass into a twinkle of moonlight.

Breaking the wax, brittle old bones. Fallen from the crumbling spine and cradled in the chest cavity: a glimmering platinum amulet, the Holy Symbol of Darkstone. The granddaughter is Helena, from The Shadow, though this sheet does not speak her name.

##### River of Ssstyx

**GM.** The river fans out into a small lake. Stone ruins dot the lake, peeking from the murky water. The ruins that stood here were overtaken by the jungle and swallowed. Across the lake a pair of stone slabs stand out of the mud, like doors, with a green gem in the middle that vaguely resembles a snake's eye.

Fire held up to the gem makes it glisten, and the doors slide open onto a stone staircase down into the dark. The steps end at a hallway heavy with moisture. Murals of overlapping snakes seem to shift whenever the eye leaves them. A dripping sounds far ahead.

Three pressure plates are in the hallway, DC 15 to find. Stepping on one shoots two darts from the snake murals, +5 to hit, 1d4 piercing and 2d10 poison, halved on a DC 14 Constitution save.

The dripping leads to a large subterranean room. Water drips through the earth and off dozens of stalactites into a lake, then trickles over the far side, where it can be heard rushing below. A single mossy stone bridge lies in ruins. Stones on each side reach toward each other and do not meet. The gap is about 15 feet. Water weirds live in the lake and attack only when something disturbs the water.

Past the gap, steep slick stairs descend beside a waterfall fed by the lake. The waterfall on the worn stone drowns out other sound. A river forms and runs ahead, with a cavern path beside it. Bleached bones come up out of the frothy rapids, and skeleton yuan-ti climb out to attack.

Farther on, the rapids slow and the river widens. It loops, then turns sharp left and falls into dark depths with no visible end. A small stone bridge still spans the place where the river seems to loop on itself. Over that short bridge is a stone altar. Snake carvings decorate the sides. On the smooth top, treasure: about 100 gold coins strewn with a few gems and trinkets, and on the pile a large golden cube.

The cube is about the size of a fist. Each of the six faces has delicate geometric patterns, interlocking lines, and angular symbols, of elven craftsmanship. The patterns seem to shift when the angle changes. The sheet describes the cube twice, in the same words. No obvious lock. Some of the carvings feel as if they could be pressed, twisted, or turned. A faint magical hum. It is a puzzle. No solution is written.

Touching the treasure throws the top of the altar aside. An undead yuan-ti chieftain rises. The water here deals necrotic damage. The sheet gives no amount.

#### First Node

Three islands, reached from a node.

**Mined island.** Small, mostly crag. Footing on the north end, then a tunnel to an open quarried middle. A flooded cavern goes deeper, propped with old water-damaged wood. Anything wild in there collapses it. A feral water elemental lives in the water. At the top of the caverns, a small wooden room. The furniture and papers are waterlogged and strewn. One note is hammered above the door. One chunk of Aureleonite is under the dirty water in that room. The sheet spells it Aurelionite. More could be mined deeper in, if the cavern holds.

**Couatl island.** A snaking island, lush on top, two small crags floating beside it. A river winds through the middle, drops down short cliffs, and falls off into mist. Couatls fly lazy between the trees and above them. Some rush in, curious, shapeshift to mimic the company for fun, and mutter Auran in their minds. Behind the tallest waterfall is the nest. Among the sleeping Couatls, piles of sticks, grasses, and leaves hold silver, gold, and crystal. If Wint is mentioned, they fetch a stone orb with white glowing lines spiraling on it.

**Node island.** Large, sparse forest and tall grass, then a sheer cliff. Below the cliff the rock is worked stone, and a huge double door is set in the wall. A small rounded basin stands in front. Smooth twirling runes arch over the doors: "Unlock these doors only if you wish to guard this portal with your life."

The key goes in. White light climbs the walls and the seam. The doors swing forward on a gust of stale air. Inside, a circular room carved in the island, a soft white glow between the floor tiles. Skeletons slump along the walls in tattered rags, old spears in their hands. They were not human. Each skull has a large beak, and bony wings lie behind them. One wears armor and sits a stone throne across the room. The doors begin to shut.

The skeletons rise and do not advance. They ready their weapons. A moment of stillness, then they charge. When the last bones drop, white light surges through the floor. The thick stone column in the center hums, shoots a beam into the ceiling, and the beam hits a giant crystal, flies into the doors, and pushes them open. It dissipates into clouds a little off the island. The clouds congeal, become a crystalline mass, and shatter outward into a rapidly spinning circle. Through it: a rough sea, a low fog, and many small lights in the distance.

As the ship pushes for the portal, figures rush past from inside the island. Masses of cloud, faces darkened with anger, and in front a human in gleaming metal armor. Streaks of white light swirl on his flesh. He flies with nothing holding him up, fists forward, one hand on a longsword, and he looks back grinning. His eyes are glowing white. They go straight for the portal.

On the far side the cloudy creatures shrink. Some pop into several smaller ones. Only the largest two keep their shape. The humanoid slows, staggers in the air, and the light in him dulls. He keeps on, straight for the lights.

That crossing is the summer of 21–22. The ritual is beaten, a node home opens for a moment, and the Auran agent who comes through near Azure Lagoon is killed at once. He names Yan-C-Bin before he dies.

## Prices

### Item Values

Gems. Glass gems are worthless. A small raw gem is 10 gp. A small cut gem is 25 gp. A fine cut gem is 150 gp. A large raw gem is 300 gp. A large cut gem is 500 gp. An intricate cut doubles the value.

Art and jewelry. Fine art or fine jewelry is 50 gp, or 150 gp. Ancient art or jewelry is 150 gp, or 100 gp and harder to sell. Extremely fancy jewelry is 1,000 gp. The sheet writes the first two as "50 gp / 150 gp" and "150 gp / 100 gp" without saying what the slash divides. Both numbers are kept.

Magic, when sold. Common 100 gp. Uncommon 400 gp. Rare 4,000 gp. Very rare 40,000 gp. Legendary 200,000 gp.

Liquid mana is 50 gp an ounce.

The GM line on this sheet is the word "null," which these exports used for an empty note. It is not a price.

### Ammunition

Compact 4 gp for 20. Medium 5 gp for 20. Long 7 gp for 10. Shell 5 gp for 5. Nitro rifle rounds 20 gp for 6. Silvering one weapon, or ten pieces of ammunition, is 100 gp at Gunther's.

### Weapon traits

**Reload.** The weapon can be fired a number of times equal to its reload score. Then spend 1 attack or 1 action to reload. Reloading takes one free hand, unless the weapon is two-handed.

**Loading.** You can fire only one piece of ammunition from it when you use an action, bonus action, or reaction to fire, no matter how many attacks you have.

**Misfire.** If the attack die is equal to or lower than the misfire score, the attack misses and the weapon cannot be used until you spend an action to repair it. Tinker's tools, Dexterity, or Intelligence if the repair is out of combat. DC 8 plus the misfire score. Fail that check and the weapon is broken. Mending it out of combat costs a quarter of the firearm's price. A creature that is not proficient increases the misfire score by 1.

**Burst.** When you take the Attack action with this weapon, you can make extra attacks equal to the burst score, as part of the same action.

**Deadly.** On a critical hit, roll the weapon's damage dice one additional time.

**Ergonomic.** Being within an enemy's reach does not impose disadvantage on ranged attacks with this weapon.

**Auto-fire.** You may attack in a cone of the listed range if you have the listed amount of ammunition loaded. Each creature in the cone makes a Dexterity save against 8 plus your attack bonus with the weapon. Failure is the auto-fire score times the weapon's damage. Success is half.

**Mounted.** The weapon must be fixed to something solid. Mounting or unmounting takes an action. Unmounted, attacks have disadvantage unless the wielder's Strength equals or beats the mounted score.

**Recoil.** After each shot, ranged attack rolls with this weapon take a cumulative penalty of the listed amount if the wielder's Strength is under the listed score. The penalty clears at the start of that creature's next turn.

### Dynamite

As an action, light a stick and throw it to a point within 60 feet. Each creature within 5 feet makes a Dexterity save, DC 10 plus the thrower's Dexterity modifier. 2d6 bludgeoning, half on a success. Sticks bound together explode at once. Each extra stick adds 2d6, to a maximum of 8d6, and 5 feet of radius, to a maximum of 20 feet. A longer fuse can be cut for 1 to 6 rounds. Roll initiative for the dynamite. It explodes on its count after that many rounds.

## Ships

Flying Boats adds a ship-combat sheet and one cargo list. Hit points and damage are scaled down by a factor of 1 to 5. Movement is on a hex grid. One hex is 50 feet, or 50 miles when the same rules are used for a day's travel. The sheet's armor line says "how hard a shit is to hit." The word meant is ship.

### Boat Basics

A ship may turn, and it typically must move at least 1 tile. Initiative is rerolled each round. Only ships and solo creatures roll. Each ship needs a captain. The captain assigns the ship's action points at the top of the round. Most actions are an ability or skill check. Characters take Action Stations on their turn to help those checks.

The default difficulty during ship combat is DC 12. The action station rolls, or the captain if no one is on that station. Weather, surprise, terrain, and a crew that is green, hurt, or afraid can move the DC.

**Strength.** The potency of the ship's attacks: the size of the weapons, or the aim of the crew. It modifies damage.

**Dexterity.** Maneuverability. Smaller ships are more dexterous. It modifies initiative and Coming About.

**Constitution.** Construction and fortitude. It is used to calculate hit points. The sheet does not give the formula.

**Charisma.** Called Skill from here on. The quality of the crew and of their leadership. It is used to hit, for Full Sail, and for Repair.

**AC.** How hard the ship is to hit. Resilience, or small size and agility.

**Speed.** How many 50-foot tiles the ship can move in a turn with favorable winds. Also the most 50-mile tiles it can travel in a day at a normal pace.

**Crew.** The minimum needed to operate without being stressed.

**Max crew.** Crew and passengers who can travel comfortably on a long voyage. A ship may carry double that number on a short trip.

**Cargo.** The number of cargo slots. The size and value of what fills a slot are the GM's.

### Ship Combat

**Captain phase.** Captains spend action points on the ship's actions. They may roll to push the crew.

**Declare stations.** Any player character may declare a station now. The bosun may try to raise morale.

**Initiative.** Lookouts, or captains, roll. Then apply the lookout and bosun modifiers.

**Turns.** Each ship acts in initiative order. On a ship's turn the people aboard may act in any order.

Each ship has its own pool of action points. The captain spends them before the turn. Anything the crew does not cover, the captain rolls. An attack action only needs a number of points at this stage. The weapons and the targets are chosen on the ship's turn.

| Action | AP | What it does | Station |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attack, broadside | 1 | Primary attack, broadside arc, range 6/12 | Gunner |
| Attack, fore or aft | 2 | Secondary attack, one die rank lower, range 3/6, any direction | Gunner |
| Repair | All | Skill test. Heal hit points equal to the primary attack's damage, or end Derelict. Only when stressed by health | Carpenter |
| Full Sail | 2 | Skill test. One extra space this turn | Rigger |
| Come About | 2 | Dexterity test. One extra rotation this turn | Rigger |
| Ram | All | If touching another ship. Each deals one hit die plus the distance traveled, and each is anchored next turn | — |
| Drop Anchor | All | Speed 0, and anchored | — |
| Weigh Anchor | All | Speed half, and no longer anchored | — |
| Cut and Run | 0 | Speed half, no longer anchored, and the ship will drift when it stops | — |
| Change ammo | 0 | Round shot, chain shot, grape shot, and the like | — |

**Stressed.** Below half hit points, or below the minimum crew. Disadvantage on attacks and on skill checks.

**Derelict.** At 0 hit points. Speed 0, no rotation, and the only action points it may spend are on Repair. The GM may drift it one space a turn, depending on weather. Further damage forces a sinking save: a d20, and 10 or higher holds. Otherwise the ship sinks. A critical hit sinks it at once. Repair on a derelict ship is at disadvantage. Success removes Derelict and restores 1 hit point, not the normal amount.

### Action Stations

Taking a station costs an action. A bonus action, a free action, and a reaction remain. Stations may be chosen again each round. A check with no player on it uses the ship's stats and no sailing die.

Most stations let the character add a d4 sailing die to one of the ship's checks.

**Captain.** One, and required. Chooses the actions. Also rolls to navigate terrain and obstacles. As a bonus action, Push the Crew: Intimidation or Persuasion. Success grants one extra action point this turn and costs one action point next turn. A captain cannot push on two turns in a row.

**Bosun.** One. May be claimed at any time, including before initiative. As a bonus action, Raise Morale: Intimidation or Persuasion, then either +1 to the ship's attacks and skill checks this turn, or the stressed condition lifted for one turn. A ship relieved that way may still repair.

**Lookout.** One. Before initiative, a character may spend an action to take it. That character rolls the ship's initiative and must add or subtract the sailing die. A ship with a lookout also has +1 AC.

**Gunner.** One per attack the ship is making. Chooses weapons and targets. Rolls the attack with the ship's Skill and adds the sailing die to the first attack. Extra gunners take turns until the attacks are spent.

**Carpenter.** No limit. Rolls Repair with the ship's Skill, at disadvantage if the ship is stressed, and adds the sailing die. If several carpenters are aboard, one rolls and each adds a sailing die.

**Rigger.** No limit. Rolls Full Sail with Skill, and Come About with Dexterity, adding the sailing die. If several riggers are aboard, one rolls and each adds a sailing die.

### Moving a Ship

A ship may move up to its full movement after the wind is applied. It must move at least 1 tile, unless it is in irons or anchored. It moves into the hex off its bow. It may rotate twice, before, during, or after the move, and no more than once in a single hex. In irons, it cannot move forward and may rotate once. Anchored, it cannot move or turn.

The point of sail is fixed at the start of the turn. A ship that starts with the wind and then turns close to the wind still has its full movement that turn.

| Point of sail | Speed |
| --- | --- |
| With the wind | Full speed |
| Close to the wind | Half speed, rounded down, minimum 1 |
| In irons | 0, and it may still rotate once |
| Anchored | 0, and it cannot turn |

Wind is a d6. The sheet gives both hex orientations.

| d6 | Flat-top hexes | Pointy-top hexes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | North | Northeast |
| 2 | Northeast | East |
| 3 | Southeast | Southeast |
| 4 | South | Southwest |
| 5 | Southwest | West |
| 6 | Northwest | Northwest |
