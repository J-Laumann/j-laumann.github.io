Prices

Ordinary supplies are one list for the whole empire. Loot values and what a western body is carrying follow.

Mundane goods

Every town sells ordinary adventuring gear, tools, mounts and tack, and trade goods. The price is the one in the fifth-edition Player's Handbook, or the Dungeon Master's Guide when that book is the price. No town sheet sets a second price for those goods.

Brass & Barter is Ironspire's storefront. Spare-a-drop Supplies, run by Springs, is Dusthaven's. The General Store handout is that same Dusthaven description and does not name another shopkeeper. Scorpion Gulch and Steamwood each have a general store of their own, with no scene written. A town that never names a shop still sells this list at these prices. Saloon menus, Gunther's silvering, church rites, train fares, and Briggs' experimental ammunition stay on their own sheets.

Item Values

Gems. Glass gems are worthless. A small raw gem is 10 gp. A small cut gem is 25 gp. A fine cut gem is 150 gp. A large raw gem is 300 gp. A large cut gem is 500 gp. An intricate cut doubles the value.

Art and jewelry. Fine art or fine jewelry is 50 gp, or 150 gp. Ancient art or jewelry is 150 gp, or 100 gp and harder to sell. Extremely fancy jewelry is 1,000 gp. The sheet writes the first two as "50 gp / 150 gp" and "150 gp / 100 gp" without saying what the slash divides. Both numbers are kept.

Magic, when sold. Common 100 gp. Uncommon 400 gp. Rare 4,000 gp. Very rare 40,000 gp. Legendary 200,000 gp.

Liquid mana is 50 gp an ounce.

The GM line on this sheet is the word "null," which these exports used for an empty note. It is not a price.

Ammunition

Compact 4 gp for 20. Medium 5 gp for 20. Long 7 gp for 10. Shell 5 gp for 5. Nitro rifle rounds 20 gp for 6. Silvering one weapon, or ten pieces of ammunition, is 100 gp at Gunther's.

Weapon traits

Reload. The weapon can be fired a number of times equal to its reload score. Then spend 1 attack or 1 action to reload. Reloading takes one free hand, unless the weapon is two-handed.

Loading. You can fire only one piece of ammunition from it when you use an action, bonus action, or reaction to fire, no matter how many attacks you have.

Misfire. If the attack die is equal to or lower than the misfire score, the attack misses and the weapon cannot be used until you spend an action to repair it. Tinker's tools, Dexterity, or Intelligence if the repair is out of combat. DC 8 plus the misfire score. Fail that check and the weapon is broken. Mending it out of combat costs a quarter of the firearm's price. A creature that is not proficient increases the misfire score by 1.

Burst. When you take the Attack action with this weapon, you can make extra attacks equal to the burst score, as part of the same action.

Deadly. On a critical hit, roll the weapon's damage dice one additional time.

Ergonomic. Being within an enemy's reach does not impose disadvantage on ranged attacks with this weapon.

Auto-fire. You may attack in a cone of the listed range if you have the listed amount of ammunition loaded. Each creature in the cone makes a Dexterity save against 8 plus your attack bonus with the weapon. Failure is the auto-fire score times the weapon's damage. Success is half.

Mounted. The weapon must be fixed to something solid. Mounting or unmounting takes an action. Unmounted, attacks have disadvantage unless the wielder's Strength equals or beats the mounted score.

Recoil. After each shot, ranged attack rolls with this weapon take a cumulative penalty of the listed amount if the wielder's Strength is under the listed score. The penalty clears at the start of that creature's next turn.

Dynamite

As an action, light a stick and throw it to a point within 60 feet. Each creature within 5 feet makes a Dexterity save, DC 10 plus the thrower's Dexterity modifier. 2d6 bludgeoning, half on a success. Sticks bound together explode at once. Each extra stick adds 2d6, to a maximum of 8d6, and 5 feet of radius, to a maximum of 20 feet. A longer fuse can be cut for 1 to 6 rounds. Roll initiative for the dynamite. It explodes on its count after that many rounds.

Accessories

Telescopic sight, 75 gp. Removes disadvantage at long range and imposes disadvantage at close range.

Bladed, 35 gp. The two-handed version of brawler. One barrel attachment at a time. 1d8 melee of the chosen type.

Suppressor, 100 gp. Compact or medium ammunition only. One barrel attachment at a time. Muzzle flash and sound are cut down. Creatures beyond half the weapon's close range do not hear it.

Hanging lantern, 25 gp. Two-handed firearms only. A lantern on the barrel: 30 feet bright, 30 feet dim, when lit.

Choke, 25 gp. One barrel attachment at a time. Shells only, and the firearm must be two-handed. Normal range doubles. Maximum range does not change. Disadvantage on ranged attacks with it applies inside 10 feet.

Fanning and levering

A feat. Requires firearm proficiency. Increase Dexterity by 1. As an action, fire up to your proficiency bonus in shots, provided the ammunition is loaded. The firearm must be a revolver or a lever-action. You fan the hammer or hip-fire the lever. Every attack roll of that action is made at disadvantage.

Ships

Flying Boats adds a ship-combat sheet and one cargo list. Hit points and damage are scaled down by a factor of 1 to 5. Movement is on a hex grid. One hex is 50 feet, or 50 miles when the same rules are used for a day's travel. The sheet's armor line says "how hard a shit is to hit." The word meant is ship.

Cloudsplitter Cargo

Filed under Characters. Fuel is 1 ton of coal an hour, and 36 tons of coal are aboard. Cargo has two slots. Both are empty. The misc line lists a Water Anchor and a Harpoon Anchor. The sheet names no owner. McMasterson is captain of the expedition's ship, and he says they also have one in Emberheim. The sheet does not say that ship is this hold.

Boat Basics

A ship may turn, and it typically must move at least 1 tile. Initiative is rerolled each round. Only ships and solo creatures roll. Each ship needs a captain. The captain assigns the ship's action points at the top of the round. Most actions are an ability or skill check. Characters take Action Stations on their turn to help those checks.

The default difficulty during ship combat is DC 12. The action station rolls, or the captain if no one is on that station. Weather, surprise, terrain, and a crew that is green, hurt, or afraid can move the DC.

Strength. The potency of the ship's attacks: the size of the weapons, or the aim of the crew. It modifies damage.

Dexterity. Maneuverability. Smaller ships are more dexterous. It modifies initiative and Coming About.

Constitution. Construction and fortitude. It is used to calculate hit points. The sheet does not give the formula.

Charisma. Called Skill from here on. The quality of the crew and of their leadership. It is used to hit, for Full Sail, and for Repair.

AC. How hard the ship is to hit. Resilience, or small size and agility.

Speed. How many 50-foot tiles the ship can move in a turn with favorable winds. Also the most 50-mile tiles it can travel in a day at a normal pace.

Crew. The minimum needed to operate without being stressed.

Max crew. Crew and passengers who can travel comfortably on a long voyage. A ship may carry double that number on a short trip.

Cargo. The number of cargo slots. The size and value of what fills a slot are the GM's.

Ship Combat

Captain phase. Captains spend action points on the ship's actions. They may roll to push the crew.

Declare stations. Any player character may declare a station now. The bosun may try to raise morale.

Initiative. Lookouts, or captains, roll. Then apply the lookout and bosun modifiers.

Turns. Each ship acts in initiative order. On a ship's turn the people aboard may act in any order.

Each ship has its own pool of action points. The captain spends them before the turn. Anything the crew does not cover, the captain rolls. An attack action only needs a number of points at this stage. The weapons and the targets are chosen on the ship's turn.

Stressed. Below half hit points, or below the minimum crew. Disadvantage on attacks and on skill checks.

Derelict. At 0 hit points. Speed 0, no rotation, and the only action points it may spend are on Repair. The GM may drift it one space a turn, depending on weather. Further damage forces a sinking save: a d20, and 10 or higher holds. Otherwise the ship sinks. A critical hit sinks it at once. Repair on a derelict ship is at disadvantage. Success removes Derelict and restores 1 hit point, not the normal amount.

Action Stations

Taking a station costs an action. A bonus action, a free action, and a reaction remain. Stations may be chosen again each round. A check with no player on it uses the ship's stats and no sailing die.

Most stations let the character add a d4 sailing die to one of the ship's checks.

Captain. One, and required. Chooses the actions. Also rolls to navigate terrain and obstacles. As a bonus action, Push the Crew: Intimidation or Persuasion. Success grants one extra action point this turn and costs one action point next turn. A captain cannot push on two turns in a row.

Bosun. One. May be claimed at any time, including before initiative. As a bonus action, Raise Morale: Intimidation or Persuasion, then either +1 to the ship's attacks and skill checks this turn, or the stressed condition lifted for one turn. A ship relieved that way may still repair.

Lookout. One. Before initiative, a character may spend an action to take it. That character rolls the ship's initiative and must add or subtract the sailing die. A ship with a lookout also has +1 AC.

Gunner. One per attack the ship is making. Chooses weapons and targets. Rolls the attack with the ship's Skill and adds the sailing die to the first attack. Extra gunners take turns until the attacks are spent.

Carpenter. No limit. Rolls Repair with the ship's Skill, at disadvantage if the ship is stressed, and adds the sailing die. If several carpenters are aboard, one rolls and each adds a sailing die.

Rigger. No limit. Rolls Full Sail with Skill, and Come About with Dexterity, adding the sailing die. If several riggers are aboard, one rolls and each adds a sailing die.

Moving a Ship

A ship may move up to its full movement after the wind is applied. It must move at least 1 tile, unless it is in irons or anchored. It moves into the hex off its bow. It may rotate twice, before, during, or after the move, and no more than once in a single hex. In irons, it cannot move forward and may rotate once. Anchored, it cannot move or turn.

The point of sail is fixed at the start of the turn. A ship that starts with the wind and then turns close to the wind still has its full movement that turn.
